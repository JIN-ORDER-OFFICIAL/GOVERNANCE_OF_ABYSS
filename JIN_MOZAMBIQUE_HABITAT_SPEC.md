## 🌴 Module 4.4: Tropical Cyclone & High-Precipitation Adaptation (JIN-IFP-TC)

Designed specifically for coastal, humid, and cyclonic emergency contexts (e.g., Northern Mozambique / Cabo Delgado corridors), this adaptation upgrades standard arid oasis protocols to manage intense hydrological loads and storm violence.

### 1. Structural Engineering & Local Materials
- **Hydraulically Stabilized Earth Blocks (CSEB):** On-site production using hand-operated mechanical block presses. Soil mixes stabilized with localized binders and hydrophobic plant-extract/lime washes to withstand horizontal wind-driven precipitation without structural degradation.
- **Double-Pitch Aerodynamic Roof Truss:** Optimized low-wind-drag roof profiles anchored to deep sub-soil footings with high-tensile bamboo/timber rafters, mitigating cyclone uplift forces while promoting rapid stormwater runoff.

### 2. Hydrological Protection & Public Health (WASH-Shelter Nexus)
- **Decentralized Roof-Catchment RWH:** Continuous food-grade guttering channeling rainwater into gravity-settled, non-electric bio-sand filtration cisterns (minimum 1,500L capacity per unit), guaranteeing clean drinking water during post-storm supply cutoffs.
- **Surface Drainage & Sub-surface Soakaway Bioswales:** Elimination of stagnant standing water through graded gravel-packed soakaway infiltration trenches positioned at each 5–10 unit cluster perimeter, directly suppressing malaria and cholera reproduction vectors.

### 3. Micro-Cluster Protective Layout
- **Radial Pod Structure:** Groups of 5 to 10 shelters organized in radial clusters facing inward toward a secure, solar-lit communal core.
- **Integrated Passive Safety:** Standalone 12V DC micro-solar nodes provide continuous perimeter illumination, eliminating unlit corridors to minimize Gender-Based Violence (GBV) risks and reinforce community self-policing.
